Pharmacovigilance

Pharmacovigilance is the disciplined process of monitoring and evaluating product safety information throughout the lifecycle of a medicine or healthcare product.

Definition

Pharmacovigilance is the science and practice of detecting, assessing, understanding, and preventing adverse effects or any other medicine-related problem. It is a core function for organizations responsible for marketed products because it ensures that safety information continues to be reviewed long after a product reaches the market.

In everyday terms, pharmacovigilance brings together case handling, signal evaluation, medical review, risk communication, and reporting discipline. It is not limited to one department or one stage of the product lifecycle; it is a cross-functional effort that supports patient safety, regulatory compliance, and informed product decision-making.

Why It Matters

Pharmacovigilance matters because medicine safety is dynamic. New patterns can emerge after a product is launched, and those patterns may have important implications for prescribing, labeling, patient counseling, or broader risk management.

For sponsors and market authorization holders, a mature pharmacovigilance system helps support timely escalation of safety concerns and better oversight of reporting obligations. It also strengthens trust with regulators, healthcare professionals, patients, and internal stakeholders by showing that safety information is being managed with discipline and transparency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is pharmacovigilance only relevant after a product is approved?

It is most visible after approval, but it begins earlier in the lifecycle. Safety monitoring can influence development decisions, trial oversight, and planning for post-approval responsibilities.

Who typically uses pharmacovigilance data?

Pharmacovigilance data is used by safety teams, medical reviewers, quality functions, regulatory professionals, and business leaders. The insights can influence both operational actions and strategic planning.

How does pharmacovigilance benefit patients?

By helping organizations detect and assess emerging safety concerns, pharmacovigilance supports better patient protection and more informed use of medicines and medical products.
