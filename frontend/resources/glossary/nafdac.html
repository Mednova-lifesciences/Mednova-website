Glossary Entry

NAFDAC

NAFDAC is the Nigerian regulatory authority responsible for overseeing products that enter the country’s health, food, and consumer markets.

Definition

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control, or NAFDAC, is the regulatory authority in Nigeria responsible for overseeing the quality, safety, and compliance of food, drugs, cosmetics, and related products. Its role is central in shaping how products are registered, assessed, and monitored for lawful entry into the Nigerian market.

For companies operating in Nigeria, understanding NAFDAC expectations is not only a compliance issue but also a practical requirement for market access and operational readiness.

Why It Matters

NAFDAC plays a direct role in the licensing, approval, and regulatory oversight of many products. Companies that engage early with local requirements can reduce delays, improve submission quality, and strengthen confidence in their market-entry strategy.

This topic naturally connects with regulatory affairs, regulatory intelligence, and NAFDAC Registration.

Key Responsibilities

NAFDAC reviews and approves product dossiers, inspections, labeling, safety information, and post-market surveillance plans. It also monitors product quality and compliance throughout the product lifecycle, enforcing measures when products fail to meet regulatory standards.

Understanding these responsibilities helps companies align their documentation, safety systems, and local operations with Nigerian regulatory expectations.

Practical Guidance

Companies should plan for regulatory timelines, local registration requirements, and the need for clear technical documentation. This includes ensuring that product information, safety data, and manufacturing details are presented in a way that reflects both global standards and Nigerian-specific requirements.

Early engagement with local regulatory experts helps reduce surprises and improve alignment with NAFDAC's processes.
