Aggregate Reporting

Aggregate reporting summarizes safety information over time so organizations can review trends, evaluate risk, and support periodic regulatory reporting.

Definition

Aggregate reporting is the structured review and summarization of safety information across multiple cases, datasets, or reporting periods. It is used to identify patterns, track trends, and present a consolidated view of safety information to internal stakeholders and regulatory authorities.

Unlike single-case reporting, aggregate reporting focuses on the broader evidence base. It helps organizations evaluate whether the observed pattern suggests a change in risk, a need to strengthen oversight, or a requirement for further assessment.

Why It Matters

Aggregate reporting is central to lifecycle safety management. It supports periodic review, helps teams understand cumulative experience with a product, and contributes to stronger decision-making around risk management and communication.

It is also closely tied to PSUR, PBRER, and post-marketing surveillance activities.

How It Works

Aggregate reporting typically combines case data, product exposure information, and safety signal assessments into a periodic review package. It may include analyses of important identified risks, emerging concerns, and any changes in the benefit-risk profile over the reporting interval.

The goal is to move beyond isolated events and provide a reasoned, consistent view of safety experience.

Practical Use

Safety teams use aggregate reports to support internal governance meetings, signal detection committees, and regulatory submissions. The reports can also be used to align stakeholders on whether a product's risk profile is stable or whether further action is required.

In regulated environments, timely and consistent aggregate reporting is a key indicator of a mature pharmacovigilance system.
