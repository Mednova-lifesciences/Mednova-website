NAFDAC Registration Preparation Guide

Preparation is the part of the NAFDAC journey that determines whether a submission is ready, defensible, and aligned with local regulatory expectations from the start.

Regulatory submission readiness begins before documents are uploaded. It requires a defined product, applicant, manufacturing chain, regulatory pathway, evidence package, and lifecycle plan. For new market-entry programmes, early alignment prevents avoidable screening failures, inconsistent data, and unrealistic launch forecasts.

Key message: the strongest programmes are built around a clear regulatory position, controlled dossiers, realistic timelines, and a plan for post-approval obligations.

Start with the regulatory position

The team should confirm the product’s classification, composition, dosage form, strength, indications, prescription status, manufacturer and sites, local applicant or agent, trademark position, and proposed supply chain. It should then map the standard, reliance, collaborative, emergency, or other applicable pathway. For some products, reliance can reduce repetition and accelerate preparation, but it is not automatic recognition. Eligibility, reference-authority evidence, local differences, and Nigerian requirements still need to be confirmed.

Build a controlled dossier

For medicines for human use, the dossier should follow the Common Technical Document structure and be managed as one evidence set across administrative information, summaries, quality, nonclinical, and clinical content where applicable. Cross-references, product names, manufacturers, batch information, specifications, analytical methods, stability data, labels, and product information should be internally consistent. Imported products also require attention to validity, authentication, and local-agent information before submission.

Documents that commonly matter

  • Application letter and completed online application
  • Evidence of Nigerian business incorporation
  • Power of attorney or manufacturing agreement
  • Manufacturing licence, free sale certificate, or Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product
  • Good Manufacturing Practice certificate and inspection documentation
  • Trademark evidence and notarised declarations
  • Product dossier, certificate of analysis, and samples
  • Proposed labels, artwork, instructions for use, or product information

Key takeaways

  • Correct classification is the foundation for a successful submission.
  • A controlled dossier is easier to review, easier to defend, and less likely to trigger avoidable queries.
  • Preparation should cover dossier readiness, inspections, samples, labelling, and launch planning.

Plan beyond the dossier

Registration can also involve GMP assessment, inspection, sampling, laboratory analysis, and product-approval review. The team should confirm manufacturing-site status, inspection history, sample permits, import arrangements, reference standards, certificates of analysis, and sample availability. Labels and patient or prescribing information should be reviewed against the dossier and Nigeria-specific requirements before artwork is finalised.

Treat published timelines carefully

Registration timelines are useful benchmarks, but they are not guaranteed commercial launch dates. Screening gaps, compliance directives, inspection scheduling, sample testing, applicant response time, and supply-chain readiness can extend the overall programme. A realistic tracker should separate preparation time, accepted-review time, agency questions, applicant-response time, inspection or laboratory activities, approval, certificate issuance, importation, and launch readiness.

Prepare for the authorised product lifecycle

The registration strategy should anticipate post-approval obligations. A registration certificate may be valid for several years, but changes may still require notification or prior approval under the appropriate variation category. Renewal, pharmacovigilance, product-quality review, labelling, advertising, distribution, and recall obligations remain relevant after approval.
