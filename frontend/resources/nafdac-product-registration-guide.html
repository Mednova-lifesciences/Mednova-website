NAFDAC Product Registration in Nigeria

Registration is a central regulatory requirement for products seeking to enter the Nigerian market. Successful preparation depends on correct classification, complete documentation, compliant labelling, and disciplined responses to regulatory queries.

Registration of regulated products is a central requirement for companies seeking to manufacture, import, distribute, advertise, sell, or use certain products in Nigeria. It enables NAFDAC to assess product quality, safety, efficacy, performance, labelling, and manufacturing compliance before market entry.

Key message: early preparation reduces delays, avoids preventable deficiencies, and improves the likelihood of a smooth review.

What NAFDAC product registration means

NAFDAC product registration is the regulatory process through which the Agency evaluates a product and determines whether it may be legally marketed in Nigeria. Registration applies to locally manufactured and imported products and differs by product category, place of manufacture, formulation, intended use, claims, risk classification, and regulatory pathway.

Products that may require registration

Regulated products include human and veterinary medicines, vaccines, biological products, medical devices, in vitro diagnostics, cosmetics, food products, supplements, herbal medicines, packaged water, pesticides, agrochemicals, and household chemical products. Correct classification is essential because it influences the reviewing directorate, evidence requirements, fees, and timeline.

The applicant and local representation

A foreign manufacturer generally requires an appropriately incorporated Nigerian applicant or local representative. The legal relationship among the manufacturer, product owner, brand owner, and applicant must be documented clearly. For imported pharmaceutical products, the power of attorney must identify the products covered and authorise the Nigerian applicant to register them.

Preparing for registration

A pre-submission assessment should confirm classification, the applicable pathway, product ownership, manufacturing-site status, document validity, dossier format, evidence supporting product claims, label compliance, inspection requirements, laboratory needs, and post-registration responsibilities. Pharmaceutical applications require particular attention to the technical dossier, while generic medicines may require bioequivalence study reports or biowaiver data.

Documents commonly required

  • Application letter and completed online application
  • Evidence of Nigerian business incorporation
  • Power of attorney or contract manufacturing agreement
  • Manufacturing licence, free sale certificate, or Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product
  • Good Manufacturing Practice certificate and inspection documentation
  • Trademark evidence and notarised declarations
  • Product dossier, certificate of analysis, and samples
  • Proposed labels, artwork, instructions for use, or product information

Key takeaways

  • Correct classification is the foundation for a successful submission.
  • Consistent manufacturer, applicant, and product information is essential.
  • Regulatory queries, inspections, and labelling issues can materially affect timelines.

The NAFDAC registration process

The registration process typically includes product classification, dossier screening, online application, document and label review, facility inspection, laboratory analysis, regulatory queries, and approval. Each step depends on complete technical and legal records and a clear response plan for deficiencies or follow-up requests.

Registration timelines

NAFDAC guidance provides indicative timelines from acceptance of a complete submission to issuance of a registration number. These periods can vary depending on submission completeness, inspection scheduling, laboratory analysis, and the speed of applicant responses. Delays are common when documents are incomplete or inconsistent.

Common causes of delay

Common problems include incorrect classification, outdated certificates, inconsistent manufacturer information, unauthenticated foreign documents, unsupported claims, noncompliant labels, poorly organised dossiers, missing stability or performance data, unclear product ownership, poor inspection readiness, and incomplete responses to regulatory queries.

Responsibilities after registration

Registration does not end regulatory responsibility. Companies must manage renewals, variations, manufacturing changes, formulation or specification changes, labelling updates, pharmacovigilance, post-marketing surveillance, quality complaints, recalls, and distributor compliance. Registration also does not automatically grant permission to advertise; separate advertisement approval may be required.
