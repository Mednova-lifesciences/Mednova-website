In-House or Outsourced QPPV in Nigeria: How to Choose the Right Model

Outsourcing changes who does the work, not who is accountable. A practical comparison of the two QPPV models NAFDAC allows, and what each one demands of your company.

By MedNova Lifesciences Last updated: October 2026 ~8 min read Decision Guide

Reviewed for regulatory accuracy by the MedNova Lifesciences pharmacovigilance and regulatory affairs team · This article summarises publicly available NAFDAC pharmacovigilance requirements for educational purposes. Companies should consult the current applicable NAFDAC regulations and guidelines when making compliance decisions.

Quick answerNAFDAC allows a Certificate of Registration Holder to either employ its own Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV) or appoint an outsourced QPPV through a service provider. Either way, the QPPV must reside and operate in Nigeria, be available permanently and continuously, and have a documented deputy. Outsourcing changes who does the work, not who is accountable: the company keeps full responsibility for its pharmacovigilance system. An in-house QPPV usually suits companies with a large Nigerian portfolio. An outsourced QPPV usually suits multinationals without a Nigerian safety team, smaller companies and new market entrants.

What NAFDAC requires, whichever model you choose

The choice between models does not change the requirements. Under the NAFDAC Good Pharmacovigilance Practice Guidelines, the QPPV must:

  • Reside and operate in Nigeria
  • Be at the company's disposal permanently and continuously
  • Have documented back-up arrangements, with a deputy who holds the information needed to act
  • Have authority over the pharmacovigilance system and the Pharmacovigilance System Master File
  • Be able to access safety data at any time, including outside working hours
  • Act as NAFDAC's single pharmacovigilance contact point on a 24-hour basis

Our guide to the QPPV in Nigeria covers each requirement in detail. The question here is only who fills the role.

Is an outsourced QPPV allowed in Nigeria?

Yes. NAFDAC's guidelines state that a Certificate of Registration Holder may subcontract activities of its pharmacovigilance system to another organisation or person, and that this may include the role of the QPPV. They also note that the QPPV details aligned to a product may be those of a contract QPPV rather than one employed directly, and that one QPPV may serve more than one company provided all obligations can be met.

The same guidelines are equally clear about the limit. The company retains full responsibility for the completeness and accuracy of the master file, and ultimate responsibility for all pharmacovigilance tasks and for the quality and integrity of the system always stays with the Certificate of Registration Holder.

The principle to rememberYou can delegate the work. You cannot delegate the accountability.

Model 1

When an in-house QPPV makes sense

  • Your Nigerian portfolio is large enough to justify a full-time safety role
  • You already run a Nigerian safety team or safety database
  • Your products carry a high safety workload, such as new molecules, biologicals or vaccines
  • You want the QPPV embedded in local commercial, medical and regulatory decisions every day
  • You can recruit and keep both a qualified QPPV and a trained deputy in Nigeria

The main weakness of the in-house model is concentration. If the safety system depends on one employee and that person leaves without a trained deputy, the company can find itself without a compliant QPPV overnight.

Model 2

When an outsourced QPPV makes sense

  • You are a foreign manufacturer with a global safety function but no team in Nigeria
  • Your Nigerian portfolio is small or mostly established products
  • You are entering the Nigerian market and need cover from the first day of registration
  • Your current QPPV has left, or is based outside Nigeria
  • You want deputy cover and inspection experience without building them yourself
  • You import or distribute several brands and need one consistent safety system

The main weakness of the outsourced model is distance. A provider can only oversee what it is told about, so the company must send safety information promptly and keep the provider involved in decisions that affect the system.

Model 3

The hybrid model

Many multinationals use a hybrid. The global safety department keeps case processing, the safety database and aggregate reports, while a Nigerian partner provides the resident QPPV and deputy, local case intake, Nigerian literature monitoring and NAFDAC liaison. This model works when the split of responsibilities is written down precisely and both sides reconcile their data regularly. Without that, cases fall between the two.

What NAFDAC requires when you outsource

NAFDAC's guidelines set specific conditions for any subcontracted pharmacovigilance work, including the QPPV role:

  • A detailed, current written contract that describes what is delegated and each party's responsibilities
  • Detailed descriptions of the delegated tasks, the interactions and data exchange, agreed definitions, tools, assignments and timelines
  • Clear arrangements for running pharmacovigilance day to day, including maintenance of any safety database
  • A way to check, on an ongoing basis, that the arrangement is being followed, with risk-based audits of the provider recommended
  • A description of the delegated services in the Pharmacovigilance System Master File, and the contract listed in its annex
  • Where the QPPV is employed by a third party, this shown in the master file together with the provider's name

The provider may itself be inspected at NAFDAC's discretion, and all of NAFDAC's pharmacovigilance guidance applies to it as it does to the company.

What a QPPV service agreement must contain

Use this checklist to review any QPPV contract before signing.

QPPV service agreement checklist

  • The named QPPV and named deputy, both resident in Nigeria
  • The products covered, listed by name and registration number
  • Each party's responsibilities, written task by task
  • How and when the company sends safety information to the QPPV, with timelines
  • Out-of-hours contact arrangements and access to the safety database
  • Who maintains the Pharmacovigilance System Master File and how changes are notified
  • Who submits individual case safety reports and periodic reports to NAFDAC
  • Signal detection, literature monitoring and risk management responsibilities
  • How NAFDAC requests and inspections are handled
  • Audit rights for the company over the provider
  • Confidentiality and data protection terms
  • Notice periods and a handover plan if the contract ends, so cover never lapses

Questions to ask a QPPV provider

  1. Where do your QPPV and deputy live and work?
  2. What pharmacovigilance and inspection experience do they have?
  3. How many companies does your QPPV currently cover, and how do you keep that manageable?
  4. How will you get to know our products?
  5. How do we reach the QPPV outside office hours?
  6. How do you handle a NAFDAC information request with a short deadline?
  7. Which other services can you provide alongside the QPPV role, such as case processing, signal detection and regulatory support?
  8. What happens to our master file and records if the contract ends?

Common mistakes with either model

  • No deputy. An in-house QPPV with nobody trained to step in.
  • A contract with no detail. An outsourced QPPV appointed by email, with no written split of responsibilities.
  • A QPPV kept out of the loop. Safety information, acquisitions and new partnerships never reach the QPPV.
  • Treating outsourcing as handing off responsibility. The company stops checking the provider's work.
  • A master file that doesn't match reality. It names one model while the company runs another.

Frequently asked questions

Can the QPPV role be outsourced in Nigeria?

Yes. NAFDAC's guidelines allow a Certificate of Registration Holder to subcontract pharmacovigilance activities, including the QPPV role, under a detailed written contract.

Who is responsible if an outsourced QPPV makes a mistake?

The Certificate of Registration Holder. Ultimate responsibility for the pharmacovigilance system always stays with the company, whoever does the work.

Does an outsourced QPPV still need to be in Nigeria?

Yes. The residency requirement applies to every QPPV, whether employed directly or provided by a third party.

Can one QPPV cover several companies?

Yes, provided the QPPV can fulfil all obligations for each pharmacovigilance system.

Is an outsourced QPPV cheaper than an in-house one?

It often is for a small or mid-sized portfolio, because the company avoids salary, deputy, training and systems costs. For a large portfolio with a heavy safety workload, an in-house team can be more economical.

Does an outsourced QPPV need to appear in the master file?

Yes. The master file must show the QPPV's details and, if the QPPV works for a third party, the name of that company. The delegated services and the contract must also be recorded.

Can NAFDAC inspect our QPPV provider?

Yes. NAFDAC may inspect the organisation to which pharmacovigilance work is subcontracted.

What happens if we change QPPV provider?

Cover must stay continuous. Plan a handover with the outgoing and incoming providers, have the new QPPV accept responsibility in writing, and update NAFDAC and the master file.
