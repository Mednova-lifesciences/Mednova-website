NAFDAC Online Registration Portal: A Practical Guide to NAPAMS for 2026

A clear starting point for companies preparing to use NAFDAC's online portal: understand who applies, what to organise before opening an application, and what follows submission.

By MedNova LifesciencesUpdated October 2026Practical guide

This guide summarises publicly available NAFDAC registration requirements for educational purposes. Confirm current portal instructions, forms, fees, and guidelines before submitting.

At a glance

NAFDAC processes drug registration applications through its NAPAMS portal. Applications are submitted by a Nigerian-incorporated applicant, with a separate application for each product. Portal submission begins a wider review process; it is not itself product approval.

Before you start

Resolve the applicant, product, and supporting documentation before beginning the online submission. For imported drug products, the preparation commonly includes:

  • Evidence that the Nigerian applicant is incorporated with the Corporate Affairs Commission.
  • A Power of Attorney from the manufacturer, or a Contract Manufacturing Agreement where the applicant owns the brand and a third party manufactures it.
  • A complete, consistent product dossier in the required format, with supporting quality, safety, and efficacy information as applicable.
  • Proposed product labels and patient information materials for review.
  • An invitation letter for the GMP inspection of the overseas manufacturing site, where required.
  • Pharmacovigilance arrangements, including QPPV and master-file information required for the registration application.

Prepare one product at a time. The published NAFDAC guidance specifies a separate application for each product. Keep product names, applicant details, manufacturer information, dossier content, and proposed labelling aligned across the submission.

How the online registration process moves

NAPAMS is the application entry point. After submission, the product moves through screening and technical review stages that include review of documents and labels, sample analysis, GMP assessment, and a registration decision.

  1. Submit the application on NAPAMSProvide the applicant and product information and upload the required supporting material. The dossier is screened and assigned a review risk tier.
  2. Respond to screening and reviewMonitor the application for requests or queries and provide complete, consistent responses within the applicable time limits.
  3. Complete document and label reviewNAFDAC reviews the submitted materials and proposed product labels. A sample-import permit follows when the relevant review is satisfactory.
  4. Submit samples and complete laboratory analysisImport and submit registration samples under the required permit and provide the supporting sample documents.
  5. Complete GMP assessment and committee reviewNAFDAC assesses the manufacturing site's GMP status. Satisfactory dossier review, GMP status, and laboratory analysis are required before committee consideration.
  6. Receive the registration decisionApproved products receive an electronic Certificate of Product Registration. Submission alone does not confer registered status.

Keep the submission on track

  • Check that authorisation documents name the products and grant the required registration authority.
  • Use one controlled source for product, manufacturer, and applicant details across the application and dossier.
  • Do not import registration samples before the required permit is issued.
  • Plan for GMP inspection and laboratory activities alongside dossier preparation.
  • Track queries and deadlines. The published imported-drug guideline gives applicants up to 90 working days to answer queries before an application closes automatically.
  • Budget for the full review pathway; portal submission or fee payment is not the same as approval.

Timeline and current requirements

NAFDAC's published guideline states a standard registration timeline of 240 working days from acceptance of a submission to issue of the registration number. The timeline can be affected by queries, dossier completeness, sample testing, and GMP inspection scheduling. Reliance pathways may have different decision targets and eligibility criteria.

Portal instructions, fees, and regulatory guidance can change. Check the current NAFDAC requirements before filing and treat published timelines as process targets rather than guaranteed launch dates.
