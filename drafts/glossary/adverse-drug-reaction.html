Adverse Drug Reaction

An adverse drug reaction is a harmful and unintended response to a medicinal product that occurs at doses normally used in humans. It is a central concept in the safety assessment of medicinal products.

Definition

An adverse drug reaction is a harmful and unintended response to a medicinal product that occurs at doses normally used in humans. It is a central concept in the safety assessment of medicinal products.

Why it matters

Understanding adverse drug reactions is important because they help organizations identify whether a product’s safety profile may be changing or whether a particular event requires closer monitoring or additional evaluation.

Real-world example

A suspected reaction reported from routine clinical use may require review so that the organization can understand whether the event is isolated or part of a broader pattern.

Related regulations and frameworks

The concept is relevant to pharmacovigilance practice and is often addressed in guidance from WHO, ICH, and regulatory authorities.

Common misconceptions

The term is sometimes treated as interchangeable with any adverse event. In practice, the distinction matters because the relationship to the product is a key part of the assessment.

Expanded frequently asked questions

What is an adverse drug reaction?

A harmful and unintended response to a medicinal product at normal doses.

Why does it matter?

Because it informs safety evaluation and can shape risk understanding.

How is it different from an adverse event?

Adverse events are wider in scope, while adverse drug reactions are specifically related to the product and the response observed.
