Signal Detection

Signal detection is the process by which new or changing safety information is identified and assessed as potentially relevant to the understanding of a product’s safety profile.

Illustration showing the concept in a life sciences context, with clear process flow and regulatory relevance.

Definition

Signal detection is the process by which new or changing safety information is identified and assessed as potentially relevant to the understanding of a product’s safety profile.

Why it matters

It matters because important patterns are not always obvious at the level of individual cases. A disciplined approach to signal detection helps organizations consider information across a wider context and determine whether it warrants further review.

Real-world example

A company may notice that similar complaints are emerging in different regions or that a particular adverse event appears with greater frequency than expected. Signal detection provides a framework for taking that information seriously and evaluating it properly.

Related regulations and frameworks

Signal detection is integral to pharmacovigilance practice and is often discussed in the context of ICH guidance, WHO references, and the expectations of regional regulators.

Common misconceptions

Some readers assume signal detection is only relevant to large safety teams. In practice, the process is relevant whenever an organization needs to monitor product safety in a structured way.

Expanded frequently asked questions

What is a signal?

A signal is information that suggests a potential safety issue or a change in the understanding of an existing one.

How is it different from case processing?

Case processing deals with individual reports; signal detection looks at patterns across information.

Why does it matter?

Because emerging patterns can point to risk that requires further evaluation.

How does MedNova relate to it?
