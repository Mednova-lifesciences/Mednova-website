Definition

Good Clinical Practice, or GCP, is an international ethical and scientific quality standard for the design, conduct, recording, and reporting of clinical trials. It is intended to protect participants and ensure the reliability of study results.

Why it matters

GCP matters because the quality of a clinical study depends on trust, consistency, and proper conduct. It creates a framework that helps teams protect participants while preserving the credibility of the evidence generated.

Real-world example

A clinical team may need to apply GCP principles throughout the study lifecycle so that documentation, oversight, and participant safety all remain aligned with agreed expectations.

Related regulations and frameworks

GCP is widely referenced by ICH and by regulators such as the FDA and EMA, and it also informs how many organizations structure their internal clinical practice.

Common misconceptions

It is sometimes seen as purely a documentation requirement. In practice, it influences the way a study is conducted and how responsibilities are handled on the ground.
