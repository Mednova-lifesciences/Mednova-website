Clinical Trial

A clinical trial is a research study designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, or effectiveness of an intervention in humans. These studies are central to the development and evaluation of therapies and other medical approaches.

Illustration showing the concept in a life sciences context, with clear process flow and regulatory relevance.

Definition

A clinical trial is a research study designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, or effectiveness of an intervention in humans. These studies are central to the development and evaluation of therapies and other medical approaches.

Why it matters

Clinical trials matter because they generate evidence that informs product development, regulatory review, and patient access. The quality of the trial process affects the reliability of that evidence.

Real-world example

A sponsor may conduct a study to assess whether a new treatment performs as expected while also collecting information about participant safety and tolerability.

Related regulations and frameworks

Clinical trials are commonly discussed within the context of Good Clinical Practice guidance, ICH requirements, and relevant regulatory standards.

Common misconceptions

People often think the term refers only to a single research phase. In reality, trials can vary in purpose, design, participant profile, and the kind of evidence they generate.
