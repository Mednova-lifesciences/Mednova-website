Definition

The Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance, or QPPV, is the role entrusted with oversight of a pharmacovigilance system and its associated responsibilities. In many regulated settings, the role carries significant accountability because it sits at the interface between safety operations and regulatory expectations.

Why it matters

The role matters because it brings focus to governance, oversight, and accountability. It is often the point at which the organization’s safety framework becomes visible to those responsible for ensuring that processes are being applied properly and consistently.

Real-world example

A company expanding into a new region may need to clarify who has final oversight of the pharmacovigilance system and how that oversight is maintained across internal teams and external partners.

Related regulations and frameworks

The role is commonly discussed in the context of broader pharmacovigilance frameworks, regional expectations, and guidance issued by authorities such as the EMA, FDA, and WHO-related reference practices.

Common misconceptions

Some people assume the role is purely symbolic. In practice, it is linked to real operational oversight and the company’s ability to demonstrate that safety processes are being managed responsibly.
