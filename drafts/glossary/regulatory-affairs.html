Regulatory Affairs

Regulatory affairs is the function through which companies engage with regulatory authorities to ensure that products meet the necessary legal, scientific, and quality requirements. It spans planning, submission preparation, compliance, and lifecycle management.

Illustration showing the concept in a life sciences context, with clear process flow and regulatory relevance.

Definition

Regulatory affairs is the function through which companies engage with regulatory authorities to ensure that products meet the necessary legal, scientific, and quality requirements. It spans planning, submission preparation, compliance, and lifecycle management.

Why it matters

The discipline matters because it helps organizations translate scientific and business goals into a workable compliance path. It supports market access, reduces avoidable delays, and helps teams manage expectations across different jurisdictions.

Real-world example

A company preparing for a new product launch may use regulatory affairs support to clarify what needs to be done, which documents are required, and how the work should be sequenced.

Related regulations and frameworks

Regulatory affairs is relevant across frameworks issued by WHO, FDA, EMA, ICH, and local agencies such as NAFDAC, depending on the product and geography.

Common misconceptions

It is sometimes treated as a purely administrative function. In reality, it can shape the company’s strategy, timing, and readiness for market entry.
