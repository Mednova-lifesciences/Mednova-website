Risk Management Plan

Definition

A Risk Management Plan is a structured approach to identifying, evaluating, and reducing risks associated with a medicinal product. It connects safety considerations with practical measures that can help manage them over time.

Why it matters

The value of a Risk Management Plan is that it helps turn safety concerns into clear, workable actions. It gives the organization a way to discuss risk in a structured and accountable manner rather than leaving it to informal interpretation.

Real-world example

A company may use a Risk Management Plan to identify a known issue, define the monitoring approach, and clarify how the team will respond if the situation changes.

Related regulations and frameworks

These plans sit within wider pharmacovigilance and risk management frameworks and are often shaped by guidance from WHO, ICH, and regulators.

Common misconceptions

Some people assume the plan is only a document. In practice, it is a framework for thinking through risk and ensuring that the organization is prepared to act.
