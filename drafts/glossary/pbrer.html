Definition

The Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Report, or PBRER, is a report that evaluates the evolving benefit-risk profile of a medicinal product over time. It is designed to bring evidence together in a way that supports ongoing assessment.

Why it matters

The report matters because it helps organizations think beyond isolated safety events and consider the broader balance of benefit and risk. That kind of evaluation is important for lifecycle oversight and for maintaining a considered view of how a product should be managed over time.

Real-world example

An organization may use a PBRER to consider how new information affects its overall understanding of the product and whether the existing safety profile remains acceptable in context.

Related regulations and frameworks

PBRERs are often discussed in connection with pharmacovigilance reporting practices and frameworks referenced by ICH, EMA, WHO, and national authorities.

Common misconceptions

It is sometimes viewed as a purely technical exercise. In reality, it can have meaningful implications for internal decision-making and long-term product strategy.

Expanded frequently asked questions

What does PBRER stand for?

Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Report.

How is it different from a PSUR?

A PBRER focuses more directly on benefit-risk evaluation, while a PSUR is more centred on cumulative safety information.

Why is it important?

Because it helps organizations assess whether the benefit-risk balance remains acceptable over time.

How does MedNova support this area?

By helping create stronger structure around the information and coordination required for reporting work.
