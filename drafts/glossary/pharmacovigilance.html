Pharmacovigilance

Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities related to the detection, assessment, understanding, and prevention of adverse effects or any other drug-related problems. It is a core discipline for organizations that need to monitor safety information responsibly across the product lifecycle.

Definition

Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities related to the detection, assessment, understanding, and prevention of adverse effects or any other drug-related problems. It is a core discipline for organizations that need to monitor safety information responsibly across the product lifecycle.

Why it matters

The discipline matters because product safety is not static. New information can emerge after a product reaches the market, and organizations must be ready to review that information carefully, escalate it appropriately, and maintain a credible process for ongoing oversight.

Real-world example

A company that launches a medication in a new territory may receive several safety reports in the first months after launch. The way the organization compiles, reviews, and interprets those reports shapes whether it can respond in a timely and responsible way.

Related regulations and frameworks

In many contexts, the topic connects with WHO guidance, ICH principles, EMA expectations, FDA practices, and local frameworks such as those relevant to NAFDAC and other regional authorities.

Common misconceptions

A common misunderstanding is that this work is only about reacting to serious incidents. In reality, it is also about creating a disciplined system for reviewing a wide range of information and maintaining a high level of operational consistency over time.

Expanded frequently asked questions

Why is this term important?

Because it underpins the way organizations identify, evaluate, and respond to safety information.

Is it the same as drug safety?

It is closely related, but pharmacovigilance is often used to describe the broader framework and discipline behind safety activities.

What kinds of organizations use it?

Pharmaceutical companies, biotech firms, CROs, MAHs, and medical device businesses can all work within this framework.

How does it relate to reporting?

It helps ensure that safety information is reviewed and communicated in a structured and appropriate way.

Why does it matter for new markets?

Because different markets can introduce different expectations, processes, and regulatory demands.

What does MedNova connect to this?

Operational support, structured safety processes, and practical guidance for oversight and reporting readiness.
