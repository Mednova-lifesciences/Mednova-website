Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR)

A Periodic Safety Update Report, or PSUR, is a regularly prepared summary of the safety information available for a medicinal product over a defined period. It provides an updated overview of the evolving safety profile.

Definition

A Periodic Safety Update Report, or PSUR, is a regularly prepared summary of the safety information available for a medicinal product over a defined period. It provides an updated overview of the evolving safety profile.

Why it matters

The value of a PSUR lies in its ability to bring together safety information in a format that supports review and ongoing oversight. It helps organizations present a clearer picture of cumulative data, changes in understanding, and the need for any follow-up action.

Real-world example

A company may use a PSUR to summarise new safety information received over a reporting interval and to show how that information fits within the broader product profile.

Related regulations and frameworks

PSURs are relevant in the context of pharmacovigilance requirements and are commonly discussed with reference to ICH, EMA, WHO, and other regulatory frameworks.

Common misconceptions

Some people assume PSURs are purely administrative. In practice, they can be an important part of how a company demonstrates that it is monitoring safety information responsibly over time.

Expanded frequently asked questions

What does PSUR stand for?

Periodic Safety Update Report.

What is it used for?

To present a structured summary of safety information over a defined period.

How is it different from a PBRER?

A PSUR focuses on cumulative safety information, while a PBRER brings the benefit-risk profile into a broader evaluation context.

Why does it matter?

Because it helps organizations maintain a clearer picture of the product’s safety profile over time.
