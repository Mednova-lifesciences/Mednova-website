Aggregate Reporting

Aggregate reporting refers to the preparation and submission of summarised safety information over a defined period. It offers a broader view of cumulative safety experience rather than focusing only on individual cases.

Definition

Aggregate reporting refers to the preparation and submission of summarised safety information over a defined period. It offers a broader view of cumulative safety experience rather than focusing only on individual cases.

Why it matters

It matters because organizations often need to communicate a more complete picture of safety information over time. The approach helps regulators and internal stakeholders understand whether trends, patterns, or changes in the risk profile require attention.

Real-world example

A company may need to present a summary of safety information gathered over a reporting interval so that stakeholders can see the broader pattern rather than only isolated reports.

Related regulations and frameworks

Aggregate reporting is commonly discussed within pharmacovigilance practice and regulated reporting frameworks such as those associated with EMA, FDA, WHO, and other authorities.

Common misconceptions

It is sometimes mistaken for a simple summary exercise. In reality, it can require careful coordination of data, quality review, and interpretation.
