Definition

An Individual Case Safety Report, or ICSR, is a report of a suspected adverse reaction associated with a medicinal product. It is one of the core data elements used to evaluate safety information and inform appropriate follow-up.

Why it matters

The importance of ICSRs lies in their role as the building blocks of safety evaluation. They carry the detail needed to understand an individual case and to decide whether it points to a broader pattern or requires additional review.

Real-world example

When a healthcare professional reports a suspected reaction, the information may need to be reviewed, assessed, and documented in a consistent way so that the case can be used reliably in safety oversight.

Related regulations and frameworks

The handling of ICSRs is connected to broader pharmacovigilance frameworks, reporting expectations, and guidance issued by authorities including the EMA, FDA, WHO, and other regional regulators.

Common misconceptions

One common misunderstanding is that ICSRs are simply paperwork. In practice, they are an important part of how safety information is processed and interpreted.

Expanded frequently asked questions

What is an ICSR?

It is a report of a suspected adverse reaction associated with a medicinal product.

Why does it matter?

Because it forms part of the evidence used in safety evaluation and decision-making.

What kind of information does it include?

It can include the patient context, product information, suspected reaction details, and supporting source data.

How is it used?

It supports both routine safety review and decision-making around emerging issues.

Is it the same as a case report form?

Not exactly. It is a specific safety report concept, though related systems may use case forms or structured intake processes.

How does MedNova relate to it?

By supporting the quality and consistency of the broader process around safety information handling.
