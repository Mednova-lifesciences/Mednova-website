Good Pharmacovigilance Practice (GVP)

Good Pharmacovigilance Practice, or GVP, refers to the principles and quality standards that guide pharmacovigilance activities. It provides a framework for ensuring that safety work is conducted consistently and responsibly.

Definition

Good Pharmacovigilance Practice, or GVP, refers to the principles and quality standards that guide pharmacovigilance activities. It provides a framework for ensuring that safety work is conducted consistently and responsibly.

Why it matters

GVP matters because it helps organizations maintain a structured approach to safety oversight. It improves the reliability of the systems in place and supports better execution of day-to-day pharmacovigilance work.

Real-world example

An organization may use GVP principles to shape how it manages case data, reviews emerging information, and applies escalation or reporting processes.

Related regulations and frameworks

GVP is widely referenced in pharmacovigilance practice and is connected to international guidance from ICH, WHO, and regulatory authorities.

Common misconceptions

It is sometimes treated as a purely theoretical framework. In practice, it influences how safety tasks are performed and how quality is sustained across the function.
