The change

One nav item moves the products out from under the services

Today the site organises everything around the three service lines. SafetyCore is reachable, but only as a sixth link in a row of service links — it reads as another service rather than as a product. Adding Technology as a peer of Services is what makes the Verify / SafetyCore product family legible to sponsors, MAHs, institutions and regulators.

Today

ServicesAboutResourcesContactSafetyCore

Typical header. 17 different link sets across 61 pages.

  • No product grouping — SafetyCore sits in the same row as service links.
  • safetycore.html carries no header nav at all, and its breadcrumb files it under Pharmacovigilance.
  • Verify has nowhere to live.

Proposed

HomeAboutServicesTechnologyResourcesContact

One header, all 62 pages. Technology is a new top-level item.

  • Services ▾ — the three service lines plus the two specialist pages.
  • Technology ▾ — MedNova Verify, MedNova SafetyCore.
  • SafetyCore is repositioned as a product and stops being an orphan page.

Naming note

Technology is a menu label, not a page. There is no /technology/ landing page in this deliverable, because the brief specifies Technology as a navigation grouping over two named products. If MedNova later wants a product-family hub for search, that is a small addition and a separate decision.

Flagged for MedNova

Seven of these eight URLs return 404 until their sprint lands. Search engines crawl declared sitemap URLs, and a run of 404s on a domain that currently ranks well for NAFDAC and pharmacovigilance terms can cost crawl budget and trust. MedNova has accepted this in order to publish the target structure now. The safer sequencing — adding each route as its page ships — remains available at any point, and the reversal is a one-line edit per route.

Implementation

What changes in the codebase

The navigation is currently duplicated inline on every page, which is how 17 variants of the same header came to exist. Rather than add an eighteenth, the link list becomes one shared component and each page's existing header shell, brand mark and call-to-action are left untouched.

The menus ship as ordinary links in the HTML, so they are crawlable and work with JavaScript disabled; the script adds the open/close behaviour, arrow-key and Escape handling, and marks the current page. Only 11 of the 62 pages collapse their header on a phone, so the menus stay inline at every width and re-anchor to the right edge on a narrow screen rather than becoming full-width blocks.

Four defects found and fixed while surveying

SafetyCore was unreachable from the header. safetycore.html carried no navigation at all — MedNova's existing product page could only be reached by following a link from elsewhere. It was indexed, but not navigable. It now carries the standard header, and its breadcrumb reads Home / Technology / MedNova SafetyCore instead of filing it under Pharmacovigilance.

Half the site was missing from the sitemap. Three copies of sitemap.xml existed in the repository, listing 30, 54 and 10 URLs — and the one actually served was the 30. Thirty-two real pages, including most of the glossary and the resource guides, were never submitted to search at all. The sitemap is now generated from each page's own <link rel="canonical">, so it cannot drift from the site again, and all three copies carry identical content: 69 URLs — 62 live pages plus the 7 Verify routes still to be built.

An unclosed footer on the capability statement. capability-statement.html was missing a </div> and a </footer>, leaving the page relying on browser error recovery. Closed. All 62 pages now balance.

Eight pages lost their in-page jump links. Pages like pv.html and cro.html had been using the site header to hold their own section anchors — QPPV, Packages, Why MedNova, FAQ. Unifying the header displaced them. Each of those eight pages now carries a secondary “On this page” nav directly beneath the header, with the original labels and targets intact.

Still outstanding

Camera access is blocked site-wide. The Permissions-Policy header sets camera=(), which will prevent the barcode and QR scanning in §5 and §8 from working at all. The fix is narrow — camera=(self) on /verify and its sub-routes only, with every other path unchanged — but it loosens a security header, so it needs MedNova's explicit sign-off rather than being bundled into a navigation change. It is listed as an open item below.

Brief §13.9

Open items

Decisions this deliverable surfaces but cannot settle.

  • MedNova

    Is “Technology” the label? Alternatives are “Platforms” or “Products”. It appears in the header of every page, so it is worth settling before the nav ships.

  • MedNova

    Does Verify get its own subdomain later? The routes here assume mednovalife.com/verify. Moving to verify.mednovalife.com after launch would mean redirects and a split SEO profile — cheaper to decide now.

  • MedNova

    Nine pages sit outside the new navigation. Consolidate them into /services/, or leave them as standalone search entry points?

  • MedNova

    Approve camera access on /verify. Scanning a barcode or QR code cannot work while Permissions-Policy sets camera=(). The proposed change is camera=(self) on /verify and its sub-routes only — same-origin, no third party, every other path untouched. Needed before Sprint 2.

  • MedNova

    Delete the two unused sitemap copies? Only frontend/public/sitemap.xml is served. The other two are now identical to it rather than contradicting it, but they remain files that look authoritative and are not.

  • NAFDAC

    Public naming of the verification service. Whether a MedNova-operated page may present itself as checking NAFDAC registration records, and in what words, affects the nav label, the page titles and the URLs themselves.

  • NAFDAC

    Falsified-medicine reporting route. Whether reports submitted at /verify/report-falsified are expected to reach NAFDAC directly, or through MedNova triage as §9 of the brief assumes.
